# Lilith Design Alteration Rules

## Document Status

**Version:** 0.1 Draft  
**Canon Stage:** Draft Canon  
**Purpose:** Define how Lilith’s design may be safely changed, tested, compared, approved, or rejected before final mascot canon is locked.

This document exists to prevent design drift while still allowing intentional refinement.

---

# 1. Core Alteration Principle

Only one major design feature should be changed at a time.

All other traits should remain as close to the current approved reference as possible.

The goal of an alteration is not to reinvent Lilith.  
The goal is to improve one specific part of Lilith while preserving her identity.

---

# 2. Current Canon Status

Lilith is currently in **Draft Canon**.

This means:

- The design is mostly established.
- Core identity traits should already be protected.
- Some details may still be refined.
- Changes must be tested before becoming official.
- Approved changes should be documented in the change log.

Once the final mascot bible is complete, Lilith moves from **Draft Canon** to **Locked Canon**.

---

# 3. Alteration Priority Order

Design changes should be tested in this order:

1. Face identity
2. Expression language
3. Body proportions
4. Hair silhouette
5. Horns and ears
6. Outfit shape
7. Jewelry and accessories
8. Color palette
9. Texture and rendering style
10. Background and presentation

Face, expression, body, and silhouette should be finalized before smaller decorative details.

---

# 4. Locked Identity Traits

These traits should not be changed during normal alterations:

- Red skin
- Black hair
- Sharp gold eyes
- Heavy upper eyelids
- Black visible lips
- Small visible fangs
- Black curved horns
- Long pointed ears
- Black nails
- Gothic black outfit
- Gold jewelry
- Compact mature-chibi body
- Mischievous smug personality
- Dark gothic comic-painterly style

Changing any of these requires a deliberate canon revision test.

# 6. One-Feature Test Rule

Each alteration test should focus on one clear question.

Good examples:

- Should Lilith’s nose be sharper?
- Should her lips be larger and more readable?
- Should her horns be thicker?
- Should her body be more compact?
- Should her eyelids be heavier?
- Should her hair cover more of one eye?

Bad examples:

- Make her look better.
- Redesign her.
- Change her face, outfit, horns, hair, and colors.
- Make a new version of Lilith.
- Make her more stylish without defining what changes.

A test is successful only when the changed feature is easy to compare against the current reference.

---

# 7. Variant Testing Format

When testing a feature, use controlled variants.

## Recommended Format

Create a small comparison set:

- **A:** Current design
- **B:** Slight change
- **C:** Moderate change
- **D:** Strong change

Example:

## Lip Shape Test

- **A:** Current black lips
- **B:** Slightly sharper lips
- **C:** Larger sharper lips
- **D:** Very sharp villainess lips

The best version may be chosen as-is or used as the basis for another refinement round.

---

# 8. Change Approval Levels

Each tested change should receive one of these statuses:

## Rejected

The change does not fit Lilith or causes too much drift.

## Needs Revision

The idea is useful, but the execution needs another pass.

## Approved for Draft Canon

The change improves Lilith and should be used in future drafts.

## Locked Canon

The change is final and should not be altered unless a major redesign occurs.
